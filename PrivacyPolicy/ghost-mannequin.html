2. Welche Daten wir verarbeiten

Ghost Mannequin erstellt aus einem von dir ausgewählten Kleidungsfoto mit KI ein Produkt- beziehungsweise Studiofoto. Du wählst das Foto aus deiner Fotomediathek; die App verwendet keine Kameraaufnahme. Vor dem ersten KI-Auftrag zeigt die App einen Hinweis zur Fotoübermittlung und bittet um deine Zustimmung.

Dabei werden auch die gewählte Hintergrundfarbe und Kleidungsart übermittelt. Es gibt kein freies Prompt-Eingabefeld. Unser Server erstellt die Textanweisung für die Bildbearbeitung; die für den Auftrag geltende Promptvorlage oder Versionskennung wird mit dem Auftrag gespeichert. Foto und Anweisung werden zur Bildbearbeitung an OpenRouter weitergeleitet. Bitte lade nur Bilder hoch, die du verarbeiten darfst, und vermeide Fotos mit erkennbaren Personen oder sensiblen Informationen.
  • Auftragsdaten: Wir speichern eine pseudonyme Nutzerkennung, eine aus Nutzerkennung und zufälliger Anforderungskennung gebildete SHA-256-Auftragskennung, Auftragsstatus und Zeitpunkte, Hintergrund- und Kleidungsoptionen, Modell- und Promptversionskennung, Bildmaße sowie Fehler- und Kostenangaben. Bildinhalte oder Provider-Anfragen werden nicht in Funktionslogs ausgegeben. Die Fotodatei wird serverseitig geprüft, gedreht und als JPEG ohne EXIF-/ICC-Metadaten gespeichert.

Diese Kennung ist pseudonym, nicht anonym: Sie kann deine Aufträge und Versuchslimits miteinander verknüpfen. 2. Data we process

Ghost Mannequin uses AI to turn a clothing photo you select into a product or studio image. You select a photo from your photo library; the app does not take camera photos. Before your first AI job, the app explains the photo transfer and asks for your consent.

  • Photo, image options, and AI instruction: The app resizes the selected photo to no more than 1,600 pixels on its longest edge, converts it to JPEG, and sends it to our backend. The selected background and garment type are also sent. There is no free-text prompt field. Our server creates the editing instruction; the applicable prompt template or version identifier is stored with the job. The photo and instruction are forwarded to OpenRouter for image editing. Upload only images you are entitled to process, and avoid photos showing identifiable people or sensitive information.
  • Job data: We store a pseudonymous user identifier, a SHA-256-based job identifier derived from that identifier and a random request identifier, job status and timestamps, background and garment options, model and prompt-version identifiers, image dimensions, and error and cost information. This identifier is pseudonymous, not anonymous: it can link your jobs and attempt limits. Firebase App Check validates app/device attestations and sends App Check tokens with backend requests. Apple or Google attestation services may be involved depending on the operating system.

Firebase Analytics may also process its own technical app/installation identifiers and automatically collected basic data, so Analytics data should not be described as anonymous. You can switch collection off in Settings; the app disables it and requests a reset of this Analytics client’s data. 5. Retention and deletion

  • Input and result images become inaccessible seven days after job creation. A cleanup task starts every 15 minutes and deletes expired images and jobs. Technical deletion occurs in the next run plus that run’s execution time, which in normal operation can mean up to about 15 minutes plus cleanup-run time; outages can delay it longer. You can delete individual results sooner. Uploads and queued jobs that have not advanced for more than one hour are removed in the next cleanup run. Stuck processing jobs are marked failed in the next run after crossing the 15-minute threshold; because of the 15-minute interval plus run time, in normal operation this can take about 15–30 minutes plus cleanup-run time.
  • Job, cost/ledger, and idempotency data are generally cleaned up 30 days after job creation. When you delete an individual job, a technical deletion record remains until that period ends. If you delete all app data, the app’s jobs, images, and ledger are removed; for at most 30 days after job creation, a hashed request value with the UID and expiry time remains to prevent the same request from being accepted as a new job. Global daily cost aggregates contain no images or user identifier and are deleted after 31 days.
  • When all app data is deleted, minimal daily and lifetime attempt counters and a deletion timestamp remain linked to the Firebase UID. They prevent deletion from resetting test and abuse limits. The Firebase Auth UID is not deleted because the Firebase project is shared with other apps and exclusive attribution of a UID to this app cannot be established safely. Google says Firebase Authentication keeps logged IP addresses for a few weeks; other authentication information may take up to 180 days to be removed from live and backup systems after the Firebase customer initiates deletion. Since this app does not delete the shared Auth UID, that provider period is not a promise that the UID is removed when app data is deleted.
  • Images exported to your photo library are not removed when app data is deleted; delete those copies in your Photos/Gallery app if you wish. The app removes its own local job data, draft photo, and image cache.
  • If you switch Analytics off, collection is disabled and the app requests a reset of client-side Analytics data. Further deletion of Analytics data already transmitted to Google is governed by Google Analytics features and retention rules.
